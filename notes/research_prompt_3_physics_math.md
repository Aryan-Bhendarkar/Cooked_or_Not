You are helping me finish a Kaggle-style computer-vision competition that **ends today at 18:29 UTC**. My current pipeline is a rank-average of zero-shot vision-language-model scores (public AUC about 0.79). I want to add **physics-based and mathematically principled signals** that are independent of appearance and robust to camera and domain shift, and I want to combine them with the VLM scores **without overfitting the public leaderboard**. CPU time is plentiful (a local machine) and I can also use 2x T4 GPUs on Kaggle.

## 1. Task
- Input: a clip = 30 consecutive dashcam frames (JPEG, 384x224, front-facing, chronological). Output: P(a crash occurs inside the clip). Metric: ROC-AUC (rank only).
- Train: 3,000 labeled clips = 1,500 pairs; each pair = a crash clip + a no-crash clip cut from the same source video. The no-crash clip is the EARLIER part (its last 10-15 frames equal the crash clip's first 10-15 frames), so train negatives are tense moments just before a crash.
- Test: 1,750 unlabeled clips (about 1/3 crashes), different cameras, places, weather and quality; test negatives are ORDINARY driving from videos with no crash. Public LB = random 30% of test (about 175 positives, noise +/-0.026 AUC), private = the other 70% and decides the rank.
- Rules: pretrained models and unlabeled test data are allowed; I must NOT hand-label test clips and must NOT try to identify the source datasets of the clips. Flag anything needing external labeled data.

## 2. What I measured
- **Models fitted on the train labels fail under the shift** (0.94 CV became 0.60 public for a DINOv2 + logistic regression model). Zero-shot VLM yes/no scoring works best: public 0.71 (CLIP), 0.75 (Qwen2-VL-2B), 0.77 (Qwen2.5-VL-7B), blends 0.79.
- **My one attempt at a motion feature failed:** a 28-number curve of the stride-2 distance between consecutive DINOv2 frame embeddings (normalised by its own mean) had train CV 0.937 but public AUC **0.462**, i.e. it carries no usable signal on test. Reasons I suspect: embedding distance mixes scene changes with camera motion; the train crash always sits in the last part of the clip (the biggest jump is late in train, uniform in test); the test videos have a period-2 zigzag in frame differences (frame-rate conversion or duplicated/interpolated frames) and are sharper than train (edge strength 9.3 vs 6.5); train has watermarks.
- Mean absolute pixel difference between neighbouring frames ramps up in train crash clips (about 6.8 to 12 over the clip) and is flat (about 7.4) in train negatives; the test average is flat and higher (about 10.4) because of sharpness, so raw motion magnitude is not transferable.
- **What we do NOT know:** whether classical physics signals (optical-flow divergence, ego-motion jerk, residual motion after global-motion compensation) separate crash from ordinary driving on these cameras. Train twin accuracy (crash clip beats its tense twin) is a sign check only, because train negatives are tense, not ordinary.
- Compute I can spare: CPU Farneback flow on 384x224 takes roughly 10 ms per frame pair, so all 1,750 test clips take about 10 minutes; a GPU RAFT/SEA-RAFT/GMFlow run takes minutes. Monocular depth (Depth Anything V2 small) and object detection/tracking (YOLO, ByteTrack) are also feasible.

## 3. Questions (give formulas, parameter ranges and code-level recipes)
1. **Looming and time-to-collision.** How to estimate time-to-collision and "looming" from optical-flow divergence, from expansion of tracked bounding boxes, or from the scale change of image patches, robustly on a moving camera with unknown intrinsics? Which statistic over time (max divergence, tau = 2/div, rate of change of 1/tau, extreme-value summary) best flags an imminent or ongoing collision while staying scale-free across cameras?
2. **Ego-motion and impact signature.** After compensating global (camera) motion with a homography, affine or essential-matrix fit (RANSAC), what residual-flow or fit-residual statistics indicate an impact or a sudden jolt (large inter-frame homography residual, abrupt rotation or translation change, camera shake energy in a high-frequency band, jerk = derivative of acceleration)? How to make these invariant to speed and to the period-2 frame-rate artifact (stride-2 flow, fps estimation, de-duplication)?
3. **Change-point and anomaly mathematics.** Which change-point or sequential tests (CUSUM, Bayesian online change-point detection, generalized likelihood ratio, extreme-value theory / peaks-over-threshold on motion features, Mahalanobis or Hotelling T2 on flow descriptors) are suited to detecting a sudden break in the motion statistics inside a 30-frame clip, and how to turn them into a calibrated, clip-level rank score that works for ordinary-driving negatives?
4. **Trajectory physics.** From object tracks (boxes, depth) or flow clusters, how to compute physically meaningful quantities such as relative closing speed, deceleration beyond human braking limits (about 9 m/s^2), lateral intrusion, overlap growth, and a Kalman-filter innovation / NIS test that flags motion inconsistent with a constant-velocity model? What are the pitfalls with a single uncalibrated dashcam?
5. **Combining physics scores with the VLM scores without labels and without overfitting.** I have no test labels. How should I standardise physics and VLM scores and combine them (rank averaging, copulas, weak-supervision label models such as Dawid-Skene or spectral methods that estimate each source's accuracy from agreement, Bayesian fusion, Fisher/Stouffer combination of p-values)? How can I check that a physics score is useful without leaking the public leaderboard (for example agreement with the VLM ensemble on test while controlling for circularity, twin accuracy on train as a sign check only, consistency across camera clusters)?
6. **Mathematics for a robust final ensemble.** Any principled tool for deciding weights and for the final choice of two submissions under public-LB noise of +/-0.026 (paired bootstrap of differences, shrinkage toward equal weights, regression-to-the-mean estimate for the private score, Bayesian model averaging)?
7. **Failure modes.** For each method above, state how it behaves when the test negatives are ordinary driving with lots of ego-motion (turns, bumps, braking in traffic), when the crash happens at the very start or end of the clip, and when the camera is mounted differently (hood, windshield, motorcycle).
