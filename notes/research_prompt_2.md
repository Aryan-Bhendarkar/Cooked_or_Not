You are helping me finish a Kaggle-style computer-vision competition that **ends today at 18:29 UTC**. I already have a strong zero-shot pipeline (public AUC about 0.79). I need techniques that raise the score on BOTH the public and the private leaderboard **without overfitting to the public one**. Please research the web (papers, Kaggle write-ups, model cards, GitHub issues) and answer the questions at the end with concrete, implementable recipes. I can run roughly two more GPU jobs of up to 3.5 hours each, so ideas must be implementable in a few hours.

## 1. Task
- Input: a clip = 30 consecutive dashcam frames (JPEG, 384x224). Output: P(a crash occurs in the clip). Metric: ROC-AUC (rank only).
- Train: 3,000 labeled clips = 1,500 pairs; each pair = a crash clip + a no-crash clip cut from the same source video (the no-crash clip is the EARLIER part; their last/first 10-15 frames are identical). So train negatives are "tense seconds before a crash".
- Test: 1,750 unlabeled clips (~1/3 crashes), different cameras, places, weather, image quality; test negatives are ORDINARY driving from videos without crashes. Public LB = random 30% of test (about 175 positives, noise about +/-0.026 AUC); private = the other 70% and decides the rank.
- Compute: Kaggle free 2x T4 (16 GB each; no bf16; fp16 overflows), 12 h sessions, internet allowed for downloads. Rules: pretrained models and unlabeled test data are allowed; I must NOT hand-label test clips and must NOT try to identify the source datasets of the clips (please do not suggest that). Flag anything needing external labeled data as "needs a rules check".

## 2. What I found (all measured)
- **Train CV is useless for choosing methods.** Models fitted on the train labels scored 0.943 CV and only 0.595 public (DINOv2 features + logistic regression); a motion-curve model scored 0.462; a "concept bottleneck" on 60 CLIP/SigLIP prompt scores scored 0.848 CV and 0.669 public; a twin-difference (pairwise) version scored 0.757 CV / 0.609 public; self-training on the unlabeled test clips (train simulation 0.683 to 0.735) scored 0.669 public, below its 0.710 seed. A scene-cluster hold-out also did not predict the leaderboard. Sharpening/blurring train frames barely changed AUC.
- **What works: zero-shot scoring with vision-language models that never saw my labels.** Score = log P("Yes") - log P("No") for the question "Does a traffic collision happen in this video - the camera car or another vehicle hitting a vehicle, person or object?" (next-token logits, no text generation). Frames are passed as a native video (8 frames per window, about 507 visual tokens), 3 windows per clip (whole clip, first half, second half), clip score = 0.5*max over windows + 0.5*mean over windows.
- Public leaderboard results (all label-free): CLIP ViT-L/14 prompt score 0.7096; Qwen2-VL-2B 0.7457; Qwen2.5-VL-7B (4-bit) 0.7717; rank-average blends 0.7860 (7B + 2B + CLIP), **0.7923** (all VLMs + CLIP, weighted) and 0.7919 (equal weights across 4 model families); two 7B models only 0.7854. So model size helps, blending different models adds about +0.015, and weights barely matter.
- **Diversity matters:** test-rank correlations between the scorers: Qwen2.5-VL-7B v1 vs v2 (different question wording and a 4th window) 0.93 (almost no new information); Qwen2.5-VL-7B vs Qwen2-VL-7B 0.67; vs Qwen2-VL-2B 0.64; vs CLIP 0.54.
- On train pairs, the 7B scorers get 82-88% twin accuracy (crash clip beats its twin) but only 0.73-0.75 AUC overall; yet test AUC is about 0.77+, because test negatives are ordinary driving. Train AUC under-estimates test AUC and cannot rank methods.
- Engineering facts: Qwen2.5-VL in fp16 gives NaN logits on T4, so I use 4-bit NF4 weights with float32 compute (bitsandbytes>=0.46.1, about 8.3 s per clip per GPU, 2 GPUs). Passing frames as a video halves cost versus 8 separate images (18.5 to 8.3 s). Frame-rate quirk: test frame differences alternate high/low every second frame (frame-rate conversion); test is sharper than train (edge strength 9.3 vs 6.5); train frames carry watermarks.
- In progress right now: Qwen3-VL-8B and 4B with the same question and windows.

## 3. Questions (answer with recipes I can implement in a few hours on 2x T4)
1. **Calibrating and combining label-free scorers.** I have 5+ weak-to-medium scorers with correlated errors and no test labels. What are the best methods to estimate each scorer's accuracy and combine them without labels (weak-supervision label models such as Dawid-Skene, FlyingSquid/spectral methods, unsupervised rank aggregation, "wisdom of the crowd" with correlated experts)? How should I standardise scores before rank-averaging (per-model z-score, rank, logit calibration, contextual calibration with a content-free input)?
2. **Improving a single VLM scorer robustly.** Which of these have solid evidence for yes/no logit scoring on video anomaly or accident detection: prompt ensembling and which kinds of wording produce *diverse* rather than redundant errors; ordinal ("rate the collision likelihood 0-9" expected value) readouts; asking about specific cues (impact, debris, sudden jolt, vehicle damage); frame count, resolution, sampling window design; test-time augmentation (horizontal flip, frame subsampling); max vs mean vs top-k aggregation across windows?
3. **Stronger or more diverse models that fit 2x T4 16 GB and a 3.5 h job** for 1,750 test clips with ~500-1,000 visual tokens per window: Qwen3-VL, InternVL3.5, MiniCPM-V 4.5 (video token compression), VideoLLaMA3, LLaVA-Video, GLM-4.1V, Keye-VL, others. For each: transformers support, token cost per 8-16 frames, fp16/T4 NaN issues and workarounds (attention implementation, scaling, quantization method), and faster inference engines that run on Turing (vLLM fp16, AWQ/GPTQ) so I can use bigger models.
4. **Pairwise or comparative VLM judgments** to refine ranking in the uncertain middle band of scores (a handful of comparisons per clip, Bradley-Terry or Elo aggregation): is there evidence that it improves AUC for ranking clips, and what is the cheapest design?
5. **Using the 3,000 labeled train clips safely.** My attempts to fit on train labels made the leaderboard worse (train negatives are near-miss clips, test negatives are ordinary driving). Is there any evidence-backed way to use them under this shift: monotone calibration of VLM scores only, few-shot in-context examples, selecting prompts, PU-style objectives, LoRA with the right loss? Or should I avoid them entirely?
6. **Not overfitting the public leaderboard.** I have only a handful of submissions left today and the final two choices must be made today. Public LB has about 175 positives. How should I choose the final two submissions under this noise (prior-based weights, shrinkage, paired bootstrap of public differences, expected private-vs-public regression to the mean)? How many near-equal candidates can I compare before the winner's public score is mainly selection bias?
7. **Frame-rate and sharpness shift** between train and test: any preprocessing for VLM inputs (deduplicating frames, temporal subsampling, light blur or sharpening) known to make VLM yes/no scores more consistent across cameras?
